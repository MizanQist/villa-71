07Climate and ground

Sun, shade and the hillside.

Guzape sits at nine degrees north on the granite hills south of Abuja's centre: a high sun all year, harmattan haze from December, rains from April to October. Drag the time of day to see how far the sun reaches under the slabs of the approach balcony.

A section through the approach balcony on the first floor, the largest of the villa's balconies at 53 m² and 3.7 m deep, with the Suite 6 balcony above it and the roof and its timber slats above that; floor heights of 3.6 m from the drawings. Sun altitude from latitude 9.1° N; the profile angle is simplified to the altitude and the slats are drawn but not counted, so the drawing is a guide to the principle, not a shading calculation.

  • Shade first

    The slabs and slats already do the work. Each balcony sits under the floor above, the top terraces under timber slats, the cars under the porte-cochère. Where glazing is exposed, extend the shading rather than tint the glass.

  • Courts and air

    Two courtyards pull air through the plan. The indoor garden and the court paths let the living room and the suites cross-ventilate when the season allows, so the air-conditioning runs less.

  • The hill

    The house steps down the slope. The garage and cinema sit in the ground below the entrance side, the pool court 1.7 m below the garden floor: earth-sheltered rooms stay cool and the levels give every terrace a view.

  • Planting

    Keep the palms, the garden courts and the planted drive. Shade over the paving and the parking bays lowers the air temperature before it reaches the walls.

  • Water

    Hold the rains on site. Permeable paving where the drive allows, controlled drainage down the slope and the water storage in the specification.

  • Energy

    Solar and generator provision, LED lighting and efficient cooling are in the specification; the flat roof beside the slats is the sunniest surface the house has.

A cool house in Guzape is mostly a matter of shade, air and the hill. The machinery comes after.

9.1° N
Latitude

The noon sun stands between 57° and 90° above the horizon, and north of overhead from May to July, so overhangs work on every side.

Dec – Feb
Harmattan

Dry north-east winds off the Sahara: hazy, low-angled light, warm days and the coolest nights of the year.

Apr – Oct
The rains

About 1,400 mm a year in convective storms, heaviest in August and September; deep slabs keep the terraces usable.

Granite
Ground

Guzape's hills are rock under shallow soil. The site falls about 1.3 m from the drive to the pool court, and the basement is cut 2.85 m into it.
